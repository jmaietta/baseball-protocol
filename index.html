Build muscle with controlled volume, moderate loads and consistent eating. Maintain sprinting, throwing and medicine-ball work.

Strength-hypertrophy

Use heavier sets while retaining enough volume to continue growing. Reduce gym volume if fall baseball activity is substantial.

Increase force production with low-to-moderate rep work. Do not grind through ugly repetitions or sacrifice throwing quality.

Cut volume, maintain strength and emphasize speed. End the planned surplus and arrive fresh, explosive and baseball-ready.

Lift twice weekly around the game schedule, fuel performance at maintenance calories and prevent unwanted weight loss.

Weeks 1–2

Establish the starting point

Collect these data before changing too much

  • Morning body weight every day; use the seven-day average.
  • Waist circumference at the navel once each week.
  • Front, side and back photos under consistent conditions.
  • DEXA, Bod Pod or consistent skinfold assessment if available through school.
  • Standing broad jump, 10-yard sprint, chin-ups and selected strength lifts.
  • Seven days of normal food intake before imposing a calorie target.
  • A preparticipation review with the athletic trainer; discuss allergies, GI issues, medications and prior injuries.

Body-fat composition changes the opening move

If the school’s qualified staff determines body fat is already above his performance range, begin near maintenance calories and pursue recomposition for six to eight weeks.

If body composition is appropriate, his waist is stable and training quality is good, begin with a modest surplus.

Do not guess solely from body weight.

Build a Florida heat plan from his sweat rate.

On representative practices, record body weight immediately before and after, fluid consumed and urine produced. As a simple starting point: about 16 oz two to three hours before, 8 oz 15 minutes before, 4 oz every 15–20 minutes during, then 16–20 oz per pound lost afterward. Avoid both large dehydration losses and forced overdrinking; the sports dietitian should individualize sodium and fluid needs.

Opening day through April

In-season strength maintenance

The job changes once games begin

Stop chasing soreness and new scale weight. Use two 35–50 minute full-body sessions per week, normally separated by at least 48 hours, to preserve strength and power. The Eckerd staff places them around games and travel. Keep 2–3 clean reps in reserve and never let a lift degrade game readiness.

Double-progression rule

  1. Select a load that allows the bottom of the rep range with two clean reps still available.
  2. Add repetitions over subsequent sessions.
  3. When every set reaches the top of the range with good form, add approximately 5 lb upper body or 5–10 lb lower body.
  4. Return to the bottom of the range and repeat.

Use a 3:1 loading rhythm off-season

After three progressive weeks, reduce working sets by roughly 40–50% for one week. Keep the movement patterns and moderate loading, but allow fatigue to dissipate.

Preseason and in-season deloads follow practice, game and travel load instead of an automatic calendar.

Stay athletic

Speed, conditioning and throwing integration

First-base speed prescription

  • Two short acceleration exposures each week in the off-season.
  • Emphasize 5–20-yard starts, including crossover and lateral-to-linear reactions.
  • Use six to eight high-quality total efforts per session.
  • Full recovery between efforts; do not turn speed work into conditioning.
  • Optional 20–30-minute easy bike or incline walk once weekly.

Throwing and field-work integration

  • Use the team’s position-player throwing and arm-care program.
  • Consolidate hard throwing and upper-body lifting when practical.
  • Practice picks, short-hop reactions and footwork while fresh, not after fatiguing conditioning.
  • Protect true recovery days.
  • Do not independently add a weighted-ball velocity program.
  • Reduce pressing if shoulder or elbow fatigue carries into throwing.

05 SafetyUse the guardrails.NCAA supplement rules, summary and evidence

Optional—not foundational

Supplement rules for a college athlete

Potentially useful

  • Creatine monohydrate: 3–5 g daily, no loading required, only if cleared by school staff.
  • Whey or casein: only for convenience—and still clear the exact product with school staff.
  • Electrolytes: useful for heavy sweating, heat or long sessions.
  • Vitamin D, iron and other micronutrients: only from dietary assessment or laboratory-guided need.

NCAA eligibility warning

The NCAA states that there are no NCAA-approved nutritional or dietary supplements and warns that products may be contaminated with banned substances not shown on the label.

Every product and medication should be cleared through the athletics department before use. Third-party testing can reduce risk but cannot eliminate it.

Avoid SARMs, prohormones, “test boosters,” proprietary muscle-building blends and unlabeled stimulant products.

The protocol in one paragraph

After a seven-day baseline, begin near 3,500–3,700 calories on training days only if that creates a modest surplus, reach 190–205 g of protein, and target 0.25–0.50 lb per week. Lift four days per week off-season, retain short acceleration work, and transition from hypertrophy to strength to power. By opening day, move to maintenance calories and two low-fatigue full-body lifts weekly. The conditional goal is 215–218 lb with stable waist, speed, power and arm health—and to preserve that useful mass through April.
